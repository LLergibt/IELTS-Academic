# Grammar
## 1. Articles (A / An / The / Zero Article)

The English article system is based on two primary concepts: countability and specificity.

  

### Definite Article (The)

Used when the listener or reader knows exactly which specific entity is being referred to.

  

- **Previous Mention / Mutual Knowledge:** The noun has been mentioned before or is clear from the context (_I bought a book and a pen. The book is expensive._).
    
      
    
- **Unique Entities:** Objects or concepts that are one of a kind (_the sun, the internet, the environment_).
    
      
    
- **Superlatives and Ordinals:** Used before superlative adjectives and sequence words (_the most important factor, the first step, the same result_).
    
      
    
- **Generic Grouping:** Used with adjectives to represent a whole class of people (_the elderly, the unemployed_).
    
      
    
- **Geography:** Required for oceans, seas, rivers, canals, deserts, mountain ranges, and plural/compound country names (_the Pacific, the Thames, the Alps, the US, the UK, the Netherlands, the Philippines_).
    
      
    

### Indefinite Article (A / An)

Used with singular, countable nouns when the specific identity is unknown to the listener or when referring to any single member of a group.

  

- **General Classification:** Introducing a singular countable noun for the first time.
    
      
    
- **Professions:** Stating someone's job (_She is an engineer. He works as a teacher._).
    
      
    
- **Rates and Frequencies:** Expressing proportion (_once a week, sixty miles an hour, five dollars a kilo_).
    
      
    
- **Quantifiers:** Used in specific fixed phrases (_a few, a little, a lot of, a number of_).
    
      
    

### Zero Article (No Article)

Used for generalizations and specific institutional rules.

  

- **General Plural and Uncountable Nouns:** Making general statements (_Water is essential for life. Computers have changed society._).
    
      
    
- **Abstract Nouns:** General references to concepts (_Education is crucial. Love is complex._).
    
      
    
- **Geography:** Continents, single countries, states, cities, individual mountains, and lakes (_Europe, Germany, Texas, London, Mount Everest, Lake Superior_).
    
      
    
- **Institutions (Primary Purpose Rule):** No article is used with _school, university, hospital, prison, church, bed_ when referring to their primary function (_He is in hospital_ [as a patient] vs. _He went to the hospital to visit a friend_ [as a visitor]).
    
      
    
- **Routine Entities:** Meals, sports, languages, and academic subjects (_eat breakfast, play tennis, study physics, speak Japanese_).
    
      
    

## 2. Prepositions of Time and Place

Prepositions dictate spatial, temporal, and logical relationships. The most common structural errors occur with _in_, _on_, and _at_, as well as dependent prepositions.

  

### Prepositions of Time

- **At (Precise points):** Exact clock times (_at 5:00 PM_), specific moments (_at midnight, at the moment, at present_), and holiday periods (_at Christmas_).
    
      
    
- **On (Days and Dates):** Days of the week (_on Monday_), specific dates (_on July 4th_), and specific days (_on New Year's Eve, on my birthday_).
    
      
    
- **In (Extended periods):** Months, years, decades, centuries, and seasons (_in August, in 2023, in the 1990s, in summer_). Also used for parts of the day (_in the morning/afternoon/evening_).
    
      
    

**Crucial Temporal Distinctions:**

  

- _On time_ = Punctual, at the arranged time.
    
      
    
- _In time_ = With enough time to spare, before it is too late.
    
      
    
- _At the end_ = Denotes a specific point in time or location (_at the end of the month, at the end of the street_).
    
      
    
- _In the end_ = Means "finally" or "in conclusion" (_We tried everything, but in the end, it failed._).
    
      
    

### Prepositions of Place

- **At (Specific Points):** Exact locations, addresses, or events (_at the door, at the bus stop, at 10 Downing Street, at a conference_).
    
      
    
- **On (Surfaces and Lines):** Flat surfaces, walls, floors, coasts, and rivers (_on the table, on the wall, on the East Coast, London is on the Thames_).
    
      
    - _Transport rule:_ Used for public transport where one can stand or walk (_on a bus, on a train, on a plane_).
        
          
        
- **In (Enclosed Spaces and Areas):** Physical enclosures, rooms, buildings, cities, and countries (_in a box, in the office, in Tokyo, in France_).
    
      
    - _Transport rule:_ Used for private transport where one must sit (_in a car, in a taxi_).
        
          
        

### Dependent Prepositions (Advanced Usage)

Certain verbs, adjectives, and nouns require specific prepositions. These do not follow logical spatial or temporal rules and must be memorized as collocations.

  

**Verbs + Prepositions:**

  

- _Account for_ (a discrepancy, a percentage)
    
      
    
- _Apologize for_ (an action) _to_ (a person)
    
      
    
- _Contribute to_ (a result)
    
      
    
- _Depend/Rely on_ (data, someone)
    
      
    
- _Insist on_ (doing something)
    
      
    
- _Succeed in_ (achieving a goal)
    
      
    
- _Result in_ (an outcome) / _Result from_ (a cause)
    
      
    

**Adjectives + Prepositions:**

  

- _Accustomed to_ (a situation)
    
      
    
- _Aware of_ (a problem)
    
      
    
- _Capable of_ (performing a task)
    
      
    
- _Interested in_ (a subject)
    
      
    
- _Responsible for_ (an operation)
    
      
    

**Nouns + Prepositions:**

  

- _Advantage/Disadvantage of_ (a method)
    
      
    
- _Cause of_ (a problem)
    
      
    
- _Decrease/Increase in_ (a number or volume)
    
      
    
- _Reason for_ (an action)
    
      
    
- _Solution to_ (an issue)
