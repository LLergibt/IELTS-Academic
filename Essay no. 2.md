**corrected by gemini:

There is a popular opinion that it is best to accept a bad situation, while there are people who argue that it is better to try and improve such situations. For me, it is absolutely obvious that, while accepting a bad situation can be helpful for **one's** mental health, it is unquestionably reasonable to try to improve **one's** current position(second person view should not be used in academic ielts texts)

Accepting a bad situation can have several **short-term benefits**(short distance pros is not academic way of writing texts) and can be helpful in many ways. When a person accepts a reality, he **is** forced to live in, **his anxiety diminishes**. **Since there is nothing he can do to change the outcome, he no longer feels pressured to take action**. This is the easy explanation to this person's problems, and with this mindset he can relax and concentrate on some other positive things. He can start to enjoy something he already has(he - has) and **appreciate** his life as it is.

While this is absolutely true and healthy for some things a person could never change, for example untreatable health issues, people **often try** to apply the same mindset **to**(apply to is the correct version) something utterly different such as an unsatisfactory job or a shortage of money. The example of this type of mistake can be seen when people are afraid to change the field of their work. It is quite common for a lot of people to struggle with finding a job they would really love. Sometimes the expectations are met with the unsatisfying reality, and when people are afraid, deciding to continue their work because of fear can result in a life spent on the job they did not want to do

Overall, **accepting an unchangeable situation caused by an unsolvable issue is both brave and reasonable**. However, in most cases where a situation can be resolved, **people should definitely try**("are" and "should" are two modal elements) to improve their lives
